COASTAL MAINE HONEY BEE FORAGE

What's Blooming?

Explore a month-by-month Maine bee forage and flowering guide, including red maple, pussy willow, clover, goldenrod and asters. Compare typical bloom periods around Ellsworth with recent community observations. Seasonal timing is a guide, not proof that a plant is flowering today.

Two different kinds of information: The plant calendar below is seasonal guidance. Recent community observations can help indicate what has been seen flowering nearby, but sightings are not verified nectar flows.

Forage indicators show reported plant resources, not guaranteed honey bee collection or nectar flow. Actual availability varies with weather, plant variety, and bee access.

Pussy willow

Salix discolor
🌼 Nectar🌾 Pollen

Typical peak: April

Possible shoulder: March–May

An early pollen and nectar resource; pollen supports brood rearing, while nectar availability depends on weather and willow plants.

Red maple

Acer rubrum
🌼 Nectar🌾 Pollen

Typical peak: April

Possible shoulder: May

Important early pollen, with nectar available when conditions allow bee flight.

Dandelion

Taraxacum officinale
🌼 Nectar🌾 Pollen

Typical peak: May

Possible shoulder: April–September

Supplementary spring pollen and nectar, especially between tree bloom periods. Flowers may recur later in the season, but late blooms are sporadic rather than a reliable nectar flow.

Apple blossoms

Malus domestica
🌼 Nectar🌾 Pollen

Typical peak: May

Possible shoulder: June

Spring nectar and pollen; bee visits also help pollinate apple flowers.

White clover

Trifolium repens
🌼 Nectar🌾 Pollen

Typical peak: June–August

Possible shoulder: September

A valuable summer nectar source, with pollen too; nectar production depends on warmth and moisture.

Basswood

Tilia americana
🌼 Nectar🌾 Pollen

Typical peak: July

Possible shoulder: June

Can produce a strong but short nectar flow where mature trees are abundant; flowers also provide pollen.

Fireweed

Chamaenerion angustifolium
🌼 Nectar🌾 Pollen

Typical peak: July–August

Possible shoulder: None noted

Provides summer nectar and pollen where stands are plentiful, especially in open or disturbed areas.

Goldenrod

Solidago canadensis
🌼 Nectar🌾 Pollen

Typical peak: August–September

Possible shoulder: July–October

A major late-season nectar and pollen source. Early October flowering is possible in mild years, but it is not dependable after frost.

Photo: Willow Coville, CC BY-SA 4.0

🌼 Nectar🌾 Pollen

Typical peak: September

Possible shoulder: August–October

Useful late-season pollen and nectar, sometimes persisting until hard frost.

🌼 NectarLong-tongued bees favored

Typical peak: June–August

Possible shoulder: September

How we select bee forage plants

This coastal Maine seasonal guide uses University of Maine Cooperative Extension resources as its starting point. Plant listings describe potential nectar or pollen resources, not proof of a current nectar flow or a recommendation to plant every species.

Native and non-native plants: Both may provide forage, but native plants are generally preferred when choosing new pollinator plantings. Plants identified as invasive in Maine should not be planted or spread, even if bees visit their flowers.
