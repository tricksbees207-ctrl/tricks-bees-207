COASTAL MAINE HONEY BEE FORAGE

What's Blooming?

Explore the flowers and trees that may support honey bees around Ellsworth, Maine, throughout the growing season. Seasonal timing is a guide, not proof that a plant is flowering today.

Two different kinds of information: The plant calendar below is seasonal guidance. Recent community observations can help indicate what has been seen flowering nearby, but sightings are not verified nectar flows.

Explore what's blooming by month

Select a month to see plants in their typical peak or possible shoulder bloom around Ellsworth. Shoulder blooms are not guaranteed; weather and frost affect timing.

Pussy willow

Salix discolor

Typical peak: April

Possible shoulder: March–May

An early pollen source for brood rearing; nectar availability varies with weather and willow plants.

Red maple

Acer rubrum

Typical peak: April

Possible shoulder: May

Important early pollen, with nectar available when conditions allow bee flight.

Dandelion

Taraxacum officinale

Typical peak: May

Possible shoulder: April–September

Supplementary spring pollen and nectar, especially between tree bloom periods.

Apple blossoms

Malus domestica

Typical peak: May

Possible shoulder: June

Spring nectar and pollen; bee visits also help pollinate apple flowers.

White clover

Trifolium repens

Typical peak: June–August

Possible shoulder: September

A valuable summer nectar source, with pollen too; nectar production depends on warmth and moisture.

Basswood

Tilia americana

Typical peak: July

Possible shoulder: June

Can produce a strong but short nectar flow where mature trees are abundant.

Fireweed

Chamaenerion angustifolium

Typical peak: July–August

Possible shoulder: None noted

Provides summer nectar and pollen where stands are plentiful, especially in open or disturbed areas.

Goldenrod

Possible shoulder: July–October

A major late-season nectar and pollen source; activity depends on weather and frost.

How we select bee forage plants

This coastal Maine seasonal guide uses University of Maine Cooperative Extension resources as its starting point. Plant listings describe potential nectar or pollen resources, not proof of a current nectar flow or a recommendation to plant every species.

Native and non-native plants: Both may provide forage, but native plants are generally preferred when choosing new pollinator plantings. Plants identified as invasive in Maine should not be planted or spread, even if bees visit their flowers.
