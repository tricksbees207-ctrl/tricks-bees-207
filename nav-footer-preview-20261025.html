WHAT DOES IT MEAN?

A botanical fingerprint

The laboratory identified plant DNA associated with holly, oak, honeysuckle, apple, daisy, clover, blackberry, cherry, and maple.

Important: These percentages represent relative abundance of identified plant DNA, not the percentage of nectar or honey contributed by each plant. DNA may come from pollen or other plant material, and identification at the genus level does not establish an exact species.

The results are one snapshot of one honey sample, not a complete record of everything our bees visited.

VARROA WATCH

Know the mite load.

Varroa monitoring is one of the most important pieces of colony health management. The chart below is built for ongoing alcohol-wash results from the apiary. As samples are added, it will show how mite pressure changes by hive over time. Read our 2026 VarroxSan oxalic acid strip placement field study involving 24 colonies across two apiaries.

🧪

Alcohol wash

A measured sample of adult bees can be washed to estimate the percentage of bees carrying Varroa mites.

📊

Track the trend

A single count is useful, but repeated sampling shows whether mite pressure is rising, falling, or responding to management.

🛡️

Protect winter bees

Late-season mite pressure matters because healthy winter bees are central to colony survival through Maine's long winter.
